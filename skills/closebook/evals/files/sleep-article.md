# Why sleep matters for memory (article excerpt)

During deep (slow-wave) sleep, the hippocampus replays recently learned information, and this replay is thought to help transfer memories to the neocortex for long-term storage. REM sleep appears to support procedural and emotional memory, and may help connect new information with older knowledge.

Studies that deprive participants of sleep after learning usually find worse recall the next day than in rested groups, even when the extra sleep happens later. A short nap after learning can give part of the benefit.

Cramming all night before an exam therefore works against itself: it trades the sleep that would consolidate earlier study for a few hours of tired, shallow reading. Spreading study over several days, with sleep in between, tends to produce better long-term retention.
